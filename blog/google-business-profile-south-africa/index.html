How to set up a Google Business Profile in South Africa (2026 guide)

By Kasisite. Published 14 October 2026, updated 14 October 2026. 8 minute read.

The short answer: A Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows your business on Google Search and Maps. Use your real business name, pick the one category that describes what you are, hide your address if you serve customers at their location, add your hours, services and photos, and ask real customers for reviews.

The three things that matter most for ranking, according to the local SEO agency Whitespark, are your primary category, a real keyword-friendly name and a steady flow of reviews.

What is a Google Business Profile and why do you need one?

A Google Business Profile is a free listing that can appear when someone searches for your business name or for what you sell. It can show your name, category, hours, website, photos, services and reviews, next to a map. For a small business it is often the first thing a new customer sees, before your website.

It does not replace a website. The profile is where people check you out. Your website is where they learn what you do and message you.

The rules that get profiles suspended

Google can suspend a profile that breaks its rules, and that removes you from Search and Maps. These are the rules small businesses break most, taken from Google's guidelines:

Google Business Profile rules that matter most for small businesses
Part of the profileWhat Google says
Business nameUse your real-world name, the same as on your sign, website and stationery. Do not add taglines, hours, phone numbers, URLs, service descriptions or location details such as "near the mall".
CategoriesUse as few as possible to describe your core business. Choose categories that finish the sentence "This business IS a". Do not use categories as keywords.
AddressIf you are a service-area business, hide your address from customers.
DescriptionDescribe your services, products and history honestly. No links of any type. Do not focus on promotions or prices.
Phone and websiteUse a number and a web address that belong to your business. The phone number must be under the direct control of the business. Do not point to pages that just refer people elsewhere, such as a social media landing page.

Storefront or service-area business: which are you?

This choice decides whether your address is shown.

  • Storefront: customers visit you. A workshop, salon or shop with set opening hours is a storefront, and it should show its address.
  • Service-area business: you go to customers, or you work remotely. Google says these businesses should hide their address and set a service area instead.

Google says a business should have one profile per location, and that a service area should not extend much beyond about 2 hours of driving time from your base, though larger areas can suit some businesses. Whitespark's view is that service areas only draw an outline on the map and do not help you rank in those places, so do not spend long on them. Your location pin still matters, even when the address is hidden, because distance affects ranking.

How to choose your categories

The primary category tells Google what your business is, and Whitespark rates it among the most important things you control. Choose the category closest to the search you want to appear for. For example, "Criminal defence attorney" will do better for that search than the broader "Law firm".

Add more categories only if they are real parts of your business. Google's rule is to use as few as possible, so do not add categories for things you do not sell.

How to set up your profile, step by step

  1. Sign in with a Google account and search for your business to claim it. If it is not there, add it.
  2. Enter your real business name and your primary category.
  3. Say whether customers visit you. If not, choose no and add your service area.
  4. Add your website and contact details. Only use details that really belong to the business.
  5. Verify the profile. Google will show the verification methods it offers for your business. Do not change your name or address while it is pending.
  6. Add hours, services, a description and photos. Start with a logo, a cover photo and real photos of your work.
  7. Ask for your first reviews from real customers.

How to get reviews the right way

  • Ask customers who are happy with the work, right after you finish.
  • Send them your review link from the profile dashboard in a WhatsApp message.
  • Ask for their honest experience, never for a star rating or specific words.
  • Reply to every review, good or bad, politely.
  • Never buy reviews or write your own. Google does not allow fake reviews, and customers can usually tell.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Adding keywords or a town name to your business name.
  • Choosing five categories when two describe you.
  • Showing a home address for a business that does not receive visitors.
  • Using a phone number that is not yours, or one you do not answer.
  • Using different business details on your website, your profile and directory listings.
  • Leaving the profile with no photos and no replies to reviews.

Does a web design business need a Google Business Profile?

Yes, but with realistic expectations. A business that works remotely and hides its address rarely shows up for "near me" map searches. The profile still makes a search for your business name show your details, photos and reviews, and it adds trust. Your website and your content do the work of bringing new searches.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a profile does not cost anything. Beware of anyone who charges you to "list" you on Google.

Can I use a Business Profile without a physical shop?

Yes. If you serve customers at their location or work remotely, you are a service-area business. Hide your address and add the areas you serve.

Can I put keywords in my business name?

Only if they are part of your real name, shown on your sign and website. Adding keywords or locations that are not part of your name can get the profile suspended.

How many categories should I choose?

As few as describe your core business. Pick one primary category that matches what you want to be found for, and add extras only if they are real parts of what you sell.

How many reviews do I need?

There is no magic number. Whitespark says to aim for a rating around 4.7 or higher and for new reviews to arrive regularly, which matters more than a one-off burst.

How long until my profile shows on Google?

It can show once it is verified, though the time depends on the verification method. Ranking for other searches takes longer, and it depends on your category, reviews and competition.
